Where Dean Stands

Issues

Four years on the council and four decades in Hermosa have shaped a clear set of priorities. Here’s where Dean stands, and what he’s pushing for next.

Public Safety & Traffic

Making Hermosa’s streets safer

Public safety and traffic are the issues Dean hears about most from residents — and ones he’s lived firsthand, both as a longtime Hermosa resident and through years chairing Redondo Beach’s Traffic, Public Works & Preservation Commission. As a CERT member and Red Cross disaster volunteer, he also brings hands-on emergency-response experience to the table.

Dean’s plan: push concrete safety fixes at the corridors and intersections residents raise most, and keep the city’s emergency-response capacity strong.

  • Prioritize traffic-calming and pedestrian-safety improvements at the intersections residents flag most often
  • Strengthen coordination between Hermosa Beach Police, Fire, and CERT volunteers on emergency preparedness
  • Support the Hermosa Beach Bicycle Masterplan to make streets safer for cyclists and pedestrians alike
  • Expand community disaster-readiness training, drawing on his own Red Cross and CERT experience
Fiscal Accountability

Real transparency at City Hall

As Hermosa heads into a period of tighter budgets, Dean believes the city has to be more disciplined and more transparent about where every dollar goes — not less. Residents shouldn’t have to dig to understand how their city is spending their money.

Dean’s plan: push for clearer public reporting on city spending, and hold new spending commitments to a higher bar.

  • Support clear, accessible public reporting on city budget decisions
  • Scrutinize new spending commitments against the city’s tightening fiscal outlook
  • Prioritize efficiency in core services over new financial obligations
  • Ensure resident and business input is heard before major fiscal decisions are finalized
Small Business & Red Tape

Cutting the friction that hurts local business

Hermosa’s small businesses are core to what makes downtown work, and Dean has focused on reducing the bureaucratic friction that makes it harder than it should be to open, run, or grow a business here.

Dean’s plan: keep cutting red tape so Hermosa’s business community can thrive.

  • Streamline permitting processes for local businesses
  • Reduce unnecessary regulatory friction that slows down small business owners
  • Support policies that keep downtown Hermosa’s business district competitive and vibrant
Environment & Coastal Protection

Protecting the coastline that defines Hermosa

Dean’s environmental commitment goes back decades — as an elected Sierra Club board member for the Palos Verdes/South Bay Group, a long-time Heal the Bay volunteer, and a Storm Water Monitor & Educator with Santa Monica Baykeeper/LA Waterkeeper. That work is personal: Hermosa’s beach and coastline are why most of us live here.

Dean’s plan: bring that hands-on environmental experience directly into city policy.

  • Advocate for clean coastal waters and beach water-quality protections
  • Apply his Sierra Club and Baykeeper experience to local environmental decisions
  • Support programs and partnerships that protect Hermosa’s beaches for the long term
Preserving Hermosa’s Character

Growth without losing what makes Hermosa, Hermosa

Dean has spent four decades in this community, including time with the Hermosa Beach Historical Society and Sister City Association. He believes growth and development shouldn’t come at the cost of the small-town beach character that brought most residents here in the first place.

Dean’s plan: balance new development with the character and history residents care about.

  • Weigh new development against its impact on Hermosa’s small-town character
  • Support preservation of community landmarks and local history
  • Keep decision-making grounded in resident input, not just developer interest
